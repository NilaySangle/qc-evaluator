# Coaching call — scoring rubric

How a coaching call is graded. Twelve dimensions, 100 points.

The three things every coaching call must strengthen are **Connection**,
**Confidence** and **Continuity**. Every score is grounded in those three
pillars and in direct transcript evidence: specific moments, quotes, or
observable behaviours.

If a behaviour is not verifiable in the transcript, score conservatively.
Never score from impressions or assumptions.

> **Scope.** Transcript only. Video delivery and in-app messaging are not part
> of this. The delivery fields inside D6 and D11 are therefore N/A, and only
> in-call promises are scored. Total is 100 points when D4 is active, 85 when
> the call had no movement coaching and D4 is switched off.

> **Discrete scoring.** Each dimension's score must be exactly one of the
> bucket values listed in its table. No interpolation. Pick the bucket the call
> most closely matches and let the reasoning carry the nuance.

> **Why this matters.** Coaching quality drives retention directly. We cut team
> churn from 5% to 2.3% in one month by improving call quality, and re-signs
> show up six months after the improvement. A missed dimension today is a churn
> risk two quarters out.

## Scoring Rubric — 12 Dimensions

### Global Automatic Score Caps

Before scoring, check these conditions. If any apply, record which cap fired and apply it:

| Condition | Cap |
|---|---|

| Coach speaks >75% of the call (client passive/monologue) | Max 75 total |

| Client struggle present but ignored or avoided | 0/5 on D8 (non-recoverable) |
| No action steps stated for either party before close | Max 70 total |

### Dimension 1 — Check-In & Connection (10 pts)

**Pillar: CONNECTION**
**SOP time allocation: 3 min**

**What to look for:** Does the coach open with genuine curiosity and gauge the client's real state before anything else? Does the call intention get set explicitly?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **10/10 — Elite** | Coach asks about body, wins, AND struggles. Listens before responding — no interruption. Reflects what they hear back to the client ("What I'm hearing is…"). States clear call intention tied to client's actual state. Coach reads what kind of call is needed today and adjusts the approach accordingly. |
| **7/10 — Strong** | Good questions but limited depth. Solid check-in, reflects but not fully. Call intention stated but generic ("let's keep making progress on your goals"). |
| **3/10 — Surface** | Surface-level: "How's it going?" Doesn't reflect. Moves to program topics within 30 seconds. No call intention stated. |
| **0/10 — Fail** | Skips check-in entirely or rushes through. No acknowledgment of client's state. Launches directly into program content. |

**SOP coach prompts to listen for:**
- "How's your body been feeling since last time?"
- "What's your biggest win since our last call?"
- "What felt hardest?"

**Call intention example (Elite):** "Let's use this call to reinforce what's working, adjust what's not, and keep you moving toward ___."

**Calibration note from Marcus's training:** "The check-in is how you gauge what kind of call this person actually needs today. The framework is a container, not a script. If someone hops on in tears, you put the framework away. Meeting them where they're at IS the framework working."

**Positive signals:** Coach adjusts call plan based on check-in answers / Client opens up beyond surface level / Intention is stated and tailored to this client's current moment.
**Negative signals:** "Okay let's get started" / Monosyllabic client response and coach moves on / No listening pause / No call intention set.

### Dimension 2 — Diagnostics Review (10 pts)

**Pillar: VALUE**
**SOP time allocation: 3–4 min (only applicable at weeks 8, 16, 24)**

**What to look for:** When applicable, does the coach demonstrate expertise through specific, personalized feedback — not generic commentary? Is 1–2 movements reviewed (not more), and are findings tied directly to client goals?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **10/10 — Elite** | Screen shares 1–2 movements only (per SOP). Makes specific, anatomically precise observations. Directly ties findings to client's stated pain points and goals: "See how your hips shift here? That's why we're building ___." Client clearly understands the connection. Coach selects movements that point back to client's why. |
| **7/10 — Strong** | Good observations but not fully tied to goals. Expertise shown, context-linking incomplete. Reviews correct number of movements. |
| **3/10 — Surface** | Reviews movements with generic feedback ("Good effort, keep your back straight"). No tie to client goals or pain points. Possibly reviews too many movements. |
| **0/10 — Fail** | Skipped, rushed, or unclear. Feedback generic or absent. Personalization not visible. |

**SOP language to listen for:**
- "See how your hips shift here? That's why we're building ___."
- "This directly connects to the ___ you mentioned."

**Calibration note from Marcus's training:** "One to two movements — not a hundred. Very specific. Always pick the movements that point back to the client's goals, their why."

**Positive signals:** Coach explicitly selects movements tied to client's presenting goal / Anatomical specificity / Client has a visible "I didn't know that" moment.
**Negative signals:** "Looks good overall" / Generic cues not connected to goals / Reviews 5+ movements without focus.

**Scoring note:** If diagnostics not applicable this cycle (non-milestone call, no video submitted), note this and score N/A — redistribute weight to D3 and D4. Do not penalize the coach.

### Dimension 3 — Program Focus + Vision (15 pts)

**Pillar: EMOTION — Belief + Long-Term Buy-In**
**SOP time allocation: 4–5 min**

**What to look for:** Does the coach connect the current block to the client's 12-month vision and identity? Or do they only talk about this week?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **15/15 — Elite** | Clearly explains what the current block targets (e.g., baseline strength, mobility, metabolic capacity). Explicitly connects this phase to client's 12-month vision by name. Reinforces Halden Method difference: "We build from your diagnostics and goals — not random workouts." Client responds with belief or insight. Client leaves understanding not just WHAT but WHY this specific block, at this specific time, is the path to their specific goal. |
| **10/15 — Strong** | Block explained and connected to goals but vision tie is generic ("this builds toward your long-term health"). Emotional resonance present but not sharp. |
| **5/15 — Mid** | Vague explanation. Block explained as logistics only. Client understands what to do this week but not why it matters long-term. No 12-month vision referenced. |
| **0/15 — Fail** | No explanation of block. Just "keep doing your workouts." No connection to vision. |

**SOP language to listen for:**
- "This block is building your ___."
- "This sets you up for your long-term goal of ___."
- "We build from your diagnostics and goals — not random workouts."

**Calibration note from Marcus's training:** "Connect back to: why did they join? What's the progress they've made? And where are they going — not just where, but WHY are they going there? Why is that important?" This is the #1 loss dimension in coaching call QC. Most coaches explain the program but forget to build the belief.

**Role play evidence (R.M. upset call):** Marcus brought an upset client back around by returning to "When you started, what were the most important things to you?" and rebuilding vision from there. Elite coaches use vision as their reset tool, not just an opening frame.

**Positive signals:** 12-month vision referenced by name / Client responds with buy-in or insight / Block tied to previous and next phase.
**Negative signals:** Only current week discussed / "Just keep going" / Client passive / Reframe not attempted when client is confused.

**Auto-cap:** No long-term vision connection anywhere in the call → max 10.

> **#1 loss dimension per Marcus's analysis:** "They explain the program but forget to make the client believe in the journey."

### Dimension 4 — Movement Coaching Quality (15 pts) — Optional

**Pillar: SUPPORT — Real Coaching, Not Commentary**
**SOP time allocation: 8–10 min**

> **Optional dimension — disable when no movement coaching occurred.**
> If the coach and client did not engage in any live movement coaching during this call (no movement performed by the client, no setup/breathing/control cues given, no in-call demonstration, no video review of movement attempts, no real-time form correction), set `disabled: true`, disabled reason to a short explanation (e.g. "no movement coaching on this call — session was entirely strategy/accountability"), and `score: null` with `band: "N/A"`. Also set `max_possible: 85` in `total_score` (see below).
>
> **Detection criteria — ALL four must be absent to disable:**
> 1. Client performed any live movement during the call.
> 2. Coach gave setup, breathing, or control cues in response to a movement.
> 3. There was a video review of a recorded movement attempt with real-time feedback.
> 4. Coach gave real-time form correction while the client moved.
>
> If even one criterion is present, score normally on the rubric below and set `disabled: false`.

**What to look for:** Does something actually improve or click during this call? Is the coach coaching — or just narrating?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **15/15 — Elite** | Reviews 1–2 movements live. Specific cues given: setup, breathing, control. Asks reflective questions: "Where do you feel this most?" / "What felt hardest here?" Improvement is observable or client verbally confirms a new understanding. Links back to goal: "This is what's moving you toward ___." If client is a "talker," coach redirects to live movement. |
| **10/15 — Strong** | Clear coaching and relevant cues. Missing reflective questions or goal link. Client engaged but no breakthrough or redirection of talker dynamic. |
| **5/15 — Mid** | Mostly telling ("Keep your back straight, squeeze your glutes"). No reflective questions. No back-and-forth coaching exchange. |
| **0/15 — Fail** | No live coaching. Just commentary or "looks fine." Client is a passive observer. Coach lets the call become purely talk without movement. |

**Reflective questions to listen for:**
- "Where do you feel this most?"
- "What felt hardest here?"
- "What changed when you tried it that way?"

**Goal link to listen for:** "This is what's moving you toward ___."

**Calibration note from Marcus's training:** "The best thing for the person who just wants to talk is probably going to be some movement. We all know that person — they love to talk a good game but they don't actually want to do the stuff that's going to get them to their goals." Elite coaches gently insist on live movement even with talkers.

**Role play evidence (T.J./Adley):** T.J. handled knee pain concern by immediately proposing: "I'm going to show you a few movements right now, just one or two, and I want us to give them a try right now on the call." Didn't spend 10 minutes talking about it.

**Positive signals:** Client has an "aha" moment / Coach adjusts cue based on client response / Live exchange (back and forth) / Talker redirected to movement.
**Negative signals:** One-way monologue / No client input solicited / Client spends 8 minutes talking and no movement happens / No goal link after coaching.

> **#3 loss dimension per Marcus's analysis:** "Coaches narrate instead of coaching. Without live exchange, reflective questions, and a goal link, the call becomes commentary — not real coaching."

### Dimension 5 — Adjustments & Strategy (10 pts)

**Pillar: GOALS — Adaptability + Confidence**
**SOP time allocation: 3–4 min**

**What to look for:** When adjustments are made — training or lifestyle — are they framed as intelligent, strategic progress? Or do they feel like a step backward?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **10/10 — Elite** | Adjustments explained with clear rationale tied to the client's long game. Explicitly framed as protection and strategy: "We're adapting — not backing off. This protects the long game." Both training and lifestyle constraints addressed if applicable. Client leaves feeling smarter and more confident, not discouraged. |
| **7/10 — Strong** | Adjustments made and explained. Framing present but brief. Client doesn't feel discouraged but isn't fully empowered. |
| **3/10 — Surface** | Adjustments made without clear rationale. Client accepts changes but doesn't understand why. Subtle discouragement possible. |
| **0/10 — Fail** | Reactive, unexplained changes. Client confused or mildly demoralized. No protective framing applied. |

**Training adjustments:** tempo, reps, volume, regressions/progressions.
**Lifestyle adjustments:** travel, stress, schedule constraints.

**SOP reframe language:** "We're adapting — not backing off. This protects the long game."

**Role play evidence (T.J./Adley):** When the client's goal shifted from handstands to muscle-building, T.J. didn't resist — he pivoted: "Now we're speaking a new language. Not too difficult, but we definitely can start to incorporate some more strength work." He reframed it as natural progression, not a failure of the original goal.

**Positive signals:** Client says "Oh, that makes sense" / Adjustment tied to the bigger goal / Coach explains what the adjustment builds toward.
**Negative signals:** "Let's reduce that for now" with no explanation / Client sounds apologetic or uncertain / No protective framing.

**Scoring note:** If no adjustments are needed this cycle, score 7/10 by default (strategic awareness is still visible in how the coach communicates the program status).

### Dimension 6 — Action Steps & Accountability (15 pts)

**Pillar: JOURNEY — Clarity + Ownership**
**SOP time allocation: 2–3 min**

**What to look for:** Do both the coach AND client leave with specific, time-bound, measurable commitments? Is verbal ownership created — not just instructions given?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **15/15 — Elite** | Coach states commitments out loud: "I'll the video tool you feedback on ___ by ___." Client commitments are specific: "Film ___ by ___." / "Complete ___ daily." Client owns a weekly theme in their own words. If client is slipping, coach creates micro-commitments. If a behavior change is noticed, coach calls it out subtly. Both sides know exactly what's expected. |
| **10/15 — Strong** | Clear commitments but lacks specific deadlines or measurability. One side more accountable than the other. Client commitment present but vague. |
| **5/15 — Mid** | Vague action steps: "Do your workouts," "Let me know how it goes." No deadline, no specific task. No verbal ownership. |
| **0/15 — Fail** | No clear next steps for either party. Call ends without accountability. |

**SOP structure to listen for:**
- Coach: "I'll the video tool you feedback on ___ by ___."  
  *(Score: whether the coach STATED this commitment live on the call — verifiable from transcript. Actual the video tool delivery is post-call and **not scored** in this QC scope — see N/A flag in JSON output.)*
- Client: "Film ___ by ___." / "Complete ___ daily." / "Message me after your next ___ session."
- Weekly theme (client's words): "This week is about ___."

**Calibration note from Marcus's training:** "We can run into this danger zone where we're trying to care more about their journey than they do. We need to consistently revisit this — getting clients to verbally commit and take ownership." Coaches must not just tell clients what to do. Verbal commitment = ownership.

**Micro-commitment protocol (when client is slipping):** "As soon as we see the change in behavior, address it immediately. What is a micro-commitment that you can make today that you will follow through on?" The 'coach notices me' effect — calling out pattern changes — is one of the highest-retention behaviors in the system.

**Role play evidence (T.J./Adley):** "Please record these movements — if you can get those back to me by Saturday, I will make sure going into next week everything is ironed out." Specific task, specific deadline, specific consequence (program adjusted).

**Positive signals:** Client repeats back their commitment / Both sides have a named task + deadline / Coach calls out a behavior pattern change / Micro-commitment created for slipping client.
**Negative signals:** "Keep doing what you're doing" / No named task / No deadline / Generic coaching around commitment / Coach never follows up on previous call's commitment.

> **#2 loss dimension per Marcus's analysis:** "Most coaches say 'great, keep it up' instead of creating owned commitments on both sides."

### Dimension 7 — Accountability Anchor (5 pts)

**Pillar: JOURNEY — Single-Point Focus**

**What to look for:** Is there a clear, non-negotiable accountability commitment the client owns for the week — one that is gated to a coach action (program progression, the video tool feedback)? This is best as ONE named anchor task, but a specific, verifiable, progression-gated deliverable the client confirms also qualifies — even when several items are requested — provided it's clear what the client owns and what it unlocks.

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **5/5 — Elite** | A clear accountability commitment the client owns and verbally confirms, gated to the coach's next action (program adjustment, the video tool feedback) — a real chain of consequence. Either form qualifies: **(a)** one explicitly-named anchor ("Send me your ___ video by Thursday — that's your accountability task this week"), **or** **(b)** a specific, verifiable deliverable the client must submit before the coach progresses ("send me these movement videos before I progress you"), even if several items are listed, as long as the deliverable and its consequence are clear and confirmed. **Time-bound** is satisfied by a hard date OR a session-relative deadline ("before our next call", "over the next two weeks before I progress you"). |
| **3/5 — Mid** | Accountability gestured at but NOT clearly gated to a coach action/consequence — tasks listed with equal weight and no clear "what this unlocks", or unclear what the client actually owns. (Several tasks are fine for Strong/Elite when they are gated to progression and confirmed — see above; "multiple tasks" alone is not a downgrade.) |
| **0/5 — Fail** | No accountability anchor. Multiple vague tasks or nothing at all. |

**SOP language to listen for:** "Send me your ___ video by Thursday — that's your accountability task this week." — OR a progression-gated equivalent: "Send me your ___ video(s) over the next two weeks; I need to see them before I progress you."

**Calibration note from Marcus's training:** The next call booking functions as a dual accountability layer. "There's that layer of accountability just by having the call in the calendar — I got to meet Dana in two weeks. I better do my stuff." The explicit weekly anchor + the booked call = dual accountability structure. Neither substitutes for the other.

**Calibration note (Devin → Owen, May 2026):** "I want you to send me some videos over the next two weeks… I need to see these before I progress you," with the client confirming ("Absolutely"), is a **satisfied accountability anchor (Strong–Elite)** even though several videos are listed and the deadline is a window rather than a single date. The deliverable is specific, verifiable, owned by the client, and gated to the coach's progression decision. Do NOT cap or downgrade purely because it was framed as a set of videos or used a session-relative deadline.

**Positive signals:** Coach names "your one accountability task is..." / A specific deliverable the client confirms ("Absolutely") / The deliverable is gated to the coach's next output (progression, feedback) / A clear submission window ("before I progress you", "over the next two weeks").
**Negative signals:** Vague commitments with no clear consequence / Client uncertain what they actually owe / No accountability deliverable stated before close / Tasks with no link to any coach action.

### Dimension 8 — Struggle Handling (5 pts)

**Pillar: CONNECTION + CONFIDENCE**

**What to look for:** When the client reveals difficulty — physical, emotional, motivational, or frustration with the program — does the coach actually coach through it, or just acknowledge it?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **5/5 — Elite** | Coach does NOT defend, prove, or take the struggle personally. Stays grounded and fact-based. Asks questions to get to the core before offering solutions. Pre-frames if client is upset, reconnects to why, reframes ("We do not stop — we shift"), goes full circle, offers options. Client leaves feeling more capable and reconnected — not just heard. |
| **3/5 — Mid** | Acknowledges struggle and offers some support. Asks some questions but doesn't fully coach through or reconnect to why. Brief or surface-level reassurance. |
| **0/5 — Fail** | Struggle ignored, minimized, avoided, or coach becomes defensive. Auto-cap: 0/5, non-recoverable. |

**SOP language to listen for:**
- "What's your why right now?"
- "How can I hold you accountable?"
- "We do not stop — we shift."

**Calibration from Marcus's live roleplay (upset client — R.M.):**
Key behaviors Marcus demonstrated:
1. **Don't use the client's negative words back at them** — Marcus didn't say "I hear that it sucks." He asked a question instead.
2. **Stay fact-based** — pulls the emotion out of the conversation.
3. **Don't defend the program** — ask questions until the client solves it themselves.
4. **Full circle close** — return to where the call started emotionally: "So from your viewpoint, is there anything I could be doing differently as your coach?"
5. **Offer options** — "Would it be more helpful to see this in writing, as a video, or talk through it on this call?" → Client chooses → Coach commits with specific timeline.
6. **Pre-frame if you know they're upset** — "What I want to do is hear you out and figure out the best path forward. How does that sound?"

**Positive signals:** Coach asks multiple questions before offering solutions / Client tone visibly shifts mid-section / Coach references original why from kickoff / Goes full circle / Offers multiple options and commits to one.
**Negative signals:** "Don't worry about it, everyone goes through this" / Defending the program / Using client's negative language / Moving on without resolution.

**Scoring note:** If NO struggle is present in this call → score 5/5 by default. Not penalized for a smooth call.

### Dimension 9 — Close Quality (5 pts)

**Pillar: CONFIDENCE**
**SOP time allocation: 1–2 min**

**What to look for:** Does the call end with emotional energy, specific celebration, and directional clarity — or just logistics?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **5/5 — Elite** | Celebrates a specific, named progress from THIS call: "The way you pushed through ___ shows you're building ___." Reiterates direction: "This block leads directly into your ___ milestone." Warm, earned close. Client leaves energized, not just satisfied. |
| **3/5 — Mid** | Positive close with some specificity but generic celebration ("You're doing great") or flat close without direction. Client leaves satisfied but not energized. |
| **0/5 — Fail** | Abrupt end. Client leaves without emotional reinforcement or directional clarity. |

**SOP language to listen for:**
- "The way you pushed through ___ shows you're building ___."
- "This block leads directly into your ___ milestone."
- "Keep up the great work. You're doing really well."

**Calibration note:** The written breakdown in Slack/the video tool that some coaches send post-call — noted in Marcus's training as a high-motivation tool ("written breakdowns motivate") — is a post-call extension, not a substitute for the in-call close. The in-call close must still land.

**Positive signals:** Celebration references something specific from THIS call / Direction linked to next phase / Client expresses enthusiasm or gratitude.
**Negative signals:** Generic "good job" / No specific event referenced / Flat tone / Close rushed because booking ran late.

> **#4 loss dimension per Marcus's analysis:** "No emotional reinforcement at the close." Common even in strong coaches.

### Dimension 10 — Next Call Booking (5 pts)

**Pillar: CONTINUITY**

**What to look for:** Is the next call booked LIVE before the call ends? Non-negotiable.

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **5/5 — Elite** | Booked live on call (non-negotiable met). Booking link shared live. Client books during the call. Date confirmed verbally: "I see you booked for ___. We're locked in." Happens before the close. |
| **0/5 — Fail** | Not booked. Call ends without next call locked in live. Automatic 0, non-recoverable. |

**SOP language to listen for:**
- "Before we wrap up, let's get our next call on the calendar so we keep this momentum going."
- "I'm dropping my booking link in the chat now — go ahead and grab a time."
- "Perfect — I see you booked for ___. We're locked in."

**Marcus's exact words from training:** "Book your next call on the call. Always. I don't care if you're at minute 29. I don't care what the situation is. 100% of the time needs to happen." Non-negotiable. Chasing people to schedule calls = wasted energy + broken accountability loop.

**Retention mechanic:** "For the client, it's an immediate next milestone — 'I've got to meet Dana in two weeks, I better do my stuff.' There's a layer of accountability just by having the call in the calendar."

**Positive signals:** Date confirmed verbally / "We're locked in" / Booking completed before close begins.
**Negative signals:** "I'll send you the link later" / Call ends without booking / "Message me when you're free."

### Dimension 11 — Continuity & Follow-Up Clarity (5 pts)

**Pillar: CONTINUITY**

**What to look for:** Does the client know EXACTLY what happens after this call ends — what the coach will do, when, and how?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **5/5 — Elite** | Coach restates the accountability anchor explicitly. States their own follow-up with specific timing: "Once you send ___, I'll the video tool you feedback by ___." Client can answer "what happens next?" without hesitation. Chain is clear: client does X by Y → coach delivers Z by W. |
| **3/5 — Mid** | Follow-up mentioned but vague timing ("I'll send you feedback this week"). Anchor partially restated. Client unsure exactly what to expect or when. |
| **0/5 — Fail** | No post-call structure. Call ends with zero continuity visible. |

**SOP language to listen for:** "Once you send ___, I'll the video tool you feedback by ___."

> **⚠️ SCOPE NOTE — the video tool delivery:** Scoring in D11 is based **only on the in-call statement** ("I'll the video tool you by ___"). Whether the coach actually sent the the video tool is post-call and **cannot be verified from the transcript**. Set `loom_delivery_verifiable: "N/A"` in output JSON. Only the presence and specificity of the in-call promise is scored.

**Positive signals:** Coach gives specific day for their deliverable / Accountability anchor restated in closing / Clear cause-and-effect chain between client action and coach response.
**Negative signals:** "I'll check in" / "Message me if you need anything" / No timeline on coach's deliverable.

### Dimension 12 — Structure & Time Management (5 pts)

**Pillar: FLOW**
**SOP target: 25–30 min**

**What to look for:** Did the call feel intentional and controlled — or scattered, rushed, or bloated?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **5/5 — Elite** | Call flows naturally through all applicable SOP sections. Pacing smooth — not rushed, not padded. Close and booking don't feel rushed. Client never confused about where the call is going. Framework is woven in, not announced. |
| **3/5 — Mid** | Slightly uneven pacing. Most sections covered. One section rushed or bloated, or 1 key section compressed to under 30 seconds. |
| **0/5 — Fail** | Disorganized. Core sections missing. Flow unclear to observer and probably to client. |

**SOP sections checklist:**
- [ ] Check-in (~3 min)
- [ ] Diagnostics review (if applicable, ~3–4 min)
- [ ] Program focus + vision (~4–5 min)
- [ ] Movement coaching (~8–10 min)
- [ ] Adjustments + strategy (~3–4 min)
- [ ] Action steps & accountability (~2–3 min)
- [ ] Close + next call booking (~1–2 min)

**Calibration note from Marcus's training:** "Don't make it robotic. You don't want to go, 'Okay, now I'm in the check-in section. Now I must move to program focus and vision.' Start to weave it into the fabric of your coaching — it'll be very natural." Robotic section announcements are a MID signal, not an ELITE signal. T.J. confirmed: the framework "shortens calls from 45–70 min to 20–26 min" without feeling scripted.

**Note:** At weeks 8, 16, 24 (diagnostic milestones), more time on D2 and D3 is expected. Pacing must be evaluated relative to call type.

**Positive signals:** Natural transitions between sections / Close doesn't feel rushed / No section noticeably absent.
**Negative signals:** Close rushed because time ran out / Accountability section skipped / Booking feels like an afterthought / Coach loses track of flow mid-call.

## Scoring Bands Reference

| Band | Score | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **ELITE** | 90–100 | Client feels seen, challenged, and connected to their future self. Referral and re-sign behavior expected. |
| **STRONG** | 80–89 | Good call with isolated weaknesses. Client satisfied but not deeply moved. |
| **INCONSISTENT** | 70–79 | Technically present but emotionally flat. Retention risk building quietly. |
| **AT RISK** | 60–69 | Weak client experience. Client may be doubting the process. |
| **FAIL** | <60 | Core elements missing. Immediate coaching intervention required. |

## Scoring Principles

1. **Three-part test for every score:** depth (how far the coach went), clarity (how well it landed), and client response (did it actually land). A perfect explanation that the client didn't confirm is not elite.
2. **Quote-first rationale.** Every rationale must begin with a direct or paraphrased reference to a specific moment in the transcript. No impressions.
3. **Conservative on missing evidence.** If a behavior cannot be verified, score the lower tier of its band.
4. **The four client feelings are the ultimate test.** Before finalizing each score, ask: would the client leave this section feeling "this is built for me / I know exactly what to do / I trust this process / my coach is paying attention"?
5. **Framework use is judged by naturalness, not completeness.** A coach who covers all sections robotically scores lower on D12 than one who weaves them organically. The framework is a container, not a script.
6. **When D4 is disabled** the call is scored out of 85, not 100. The percentage is the raw score over 85. Report the result on the 100 scale.
