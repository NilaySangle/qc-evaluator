# Kick-off call — scoring rubric

How a kick-off call is graded. Twelve dimensions, 100 points.

Every score is grounded in direct transcript evidence: specific moments,
quotes, or observable behaviours. Never score from assumptions or general
impressions. If a behaviour is not verifiable in the transcript, score
conservatively.

> **Scope.** Transcript only, all twelve dimensions active. D7 (support system
> clarity) and D12 (post-call execution) are scored on what is communicated
> during the call, not on whether the follow-up actually landed afterwards.

> **Band-based scoring.** Each dimension's score must fall inside one of the
> bands listed in its table. Within a band, any integer works (or a half step
> where the dimension's max is 5 or less) depending on how close the call sits
> to the next band up. A call that clearly exceeds the Mid criteria but does not
> fully meet Elite belongs in the Strong band. Rounding down to Mid needs a
> stated reason.

## Scoring Rubric — 12 Dimensions

### Global Automatic Score Caps

Before scoring, check these conditions. If any apply, record which cap fired and apply it:

| Condition | Cap |
|---|---|
| No follow-up questions anywhere in the call | Max 70 total |
| Coach speaks >70% of the time without client engagement | Max 80 total |
| Client shows unresolved confusion at any point | Max 75 total |
| No North Star statement constructed | Max 10/15 on Dimension 4 |

### Dimension 1 — Pre-Call Preparation (10 pts)

**What to look for:** Does the coach demonstrate they reviewed the sales notes BEFORE the call? Do they reference the client's name, goals, injuries, and context without asking?

> **Score on conduct, not on disclosure.** Credit preparation when the coach demonstrably uses information that could only have come from the sales notes — specific goals, named pain areas, history details, lifestyle context — even if the coach never explicitly says "I read your notes" or equivalent. The verbal acknowledgement is a positive signal, not a requirement.

| Band | Score | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **Elite** | 9–10 | Fully reviewed intake, references goals naturally, no repetition. References specific goals + name + injuries within the first 2 minutes. Uses at least 2 specific details from CRM/notes naturally and in passing. May (but need not) explicitly tell client they don't need to repeat the sales call. Score 10 when delivery is seamless and at least one verbal acknowledgement is present; 9 when otherwise elite without the verbal acknowledgement. |
| **Strong** | 6–8 | Clear evidence of preparation in the content (specific goals, pain areas, or context from the sales call surfaced naturally), but with a small gap: a single factual misstep, slightly delayed reference, or one redundant question. Allowing the client to voluntarily share context for relational warmth is NOT a deduction. Score 8 when the gap is minor and clearly outweighed by solid prep; 6 when prep is real but uneven. |
| **Mid** | 4–5 | Partial preparation. Some notes used, but several redundant questions or a generic, low-personalization intro. Reads notes mechanically. Reserve for cases where prep is visibly thin, not for cases where prep is solid but the coach didn't verbally announce it. |
| **Weak** | 1–3 | Minimal preparation visible. One or two surface references at most; client does most of the context-setting. |
| **Fail** | 0 | Clearly unprepared. Asks client their name or what brought them here. Completely resets the sale. |

**Positive transcript signals:** Specific goals/pain/history surfaced in the opening / "I saw from your notes that…" / "Sasha mentioned that…" / "You don't need to repeat what you told the team…"  
**Negative signals:** "Can you tell me about yourself?" / "What brought you here?" / Long pause while searching for information / Multiple redundant logistics questions already covered in sales notes.

> **Calibration note:** Do NOT default to Mid (4–5) simply because the coach did not say "I read your notes." Do NOT drop a strong call to Mid for a single misstep (e.g. wrong city) when the rest of the preparation is solid — score 6–8 instead.

### Dimension 2 — Rapport & Tone (10 pts)

**What to look for:** Does a genuine human connection form? Does the coach adapt their energy to the client? Does the client open up?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **10/10 — Elite** | Warm, calm, personalized, matches client energy. Conversation is natural and non-scripted. Uses client's name organically. Shares something personal and relevant. Client opens up spontaneously with personal stories. |
| **7/10 — Strong** | Friendly but surface-level. Warm and conversational but not deeply personal. Good connection but little emotional mirroring or depth. |
| **3/10 — Mid** | Mechanical / scripted feel. Friendly but transactional. Light conversation without real connection. |
| **0/10 — Fail** | Cold, rushed, transactional. Skips rapport entirely. No attempt at personal connection. Client gives monosyllabic answers. |

**Positive signals:** Client shares personal stories unprompted / Natural laughter / Client says "I like that you…"  
**Negative signals:** Awkward silences / Monosyllabic client responses / Coach talks about themselves excessively.

### Dimension 3 — Agenda Framing (5 pts)

**What to look for:** Does the coach take control of the call structure upfront and communicate what will happen?

> **Numbered enumeration is NOT required.** A sequenced delivery that covers at least three distinct phases of the call (e.g. "first… then… and finally…", or comma/and-connected items) qualifies as structured if it is paired with explicit time framing and at least implicit client buy-in.

| Band | Score | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **Elite** | 4.5–5 | Clear agenda with explicit time framing AND ≥3 sequenced phases (numbered or natural-language) AND client verbal consent ("sounds good"). Score 5 when all three elements are crisp; 4.5 when the agenda is fully present and sequenced but slightly informal in delivery. |
| **Mid** | 2.5–3.5 | Agenda mentioned but partial — either time framing missing, fewer than 3 phases, or no client buy-in. Implicit structure rather than intentional framing. |
| **Weak** | 1–2 | Brief or fragmented mention of what's coming, without sequencing or framing. |
| **Fail** | 0 | No upfront structure. Launches into random topics. Client has no sense of where the call is going. |

**Positive signals:** Time stated ("we've got 30 minutes") / Sequenced coverage ("connect, get aligned on your goals, walk you through the journey, support, schedule next") — numbered or natural-language / Client confirms ("sounds good").  
**Negative signals:** Starts with random questions / Client doesn't know where the call is going / No time stated / Single vague mention without sequencing.

> **Calibration note:** A coach who says "we've got 30 minutes — connect, get aligned on your goals, what success looks like, walk you through the journey, get clear on support, and schedule the next call" is delivering Elite-level agenda framing even without numbered enumeration. Score 4.5–5, not 3.

### Dimension 4 — Goal Alignment & Deep Why (15 pts)

**What to look for:** Does the coach go beyond functional goals to uncover the emotional/identity driver? Is a North Star statement built?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **15/15 — Elite** | Extracts emotional drivers + clear 30-day success metrics. ≥2 follow-up questions on "why". Extracts emotional/identity driver (fear, family, legacy, career, self-image). Builds a North Star statement: "What I hear you saying is you want to be the active husband and father you envision for decades to come." Defines specific 30-day success metric. Client verbally confirms ("Yes, that's exactly it"). |
| **10/15 — Strong** | Understands goals but stays surface level. 1 follow-up + some emotional context. 30-day goal vague or not established. North Star implied but not solidified. Physical goals identified, not emotionally deep. |
| **5/15 — Mid** | Mostly repeats sales notes. Asks "what are your goals?" and accepts the first answer. Stays physical only ("lose weight", "more flexible"). No probing, no emotional depth, no North Star. |
| **0/15 — Fail** | No meaningful alignment. Reads from sales notes without engagement. Skips or rushes. Accepts generic answer ("I want to be healthy") and moves on. |

**Positive signals:** "Why is that important to you?" / "How would that impact your life?" / "What would happen if nothing changes?" / "What I hear you saying is…" / "What would make day 30 feel like the best investment you've ever made?"  
**Negative signals:** Accepts "I want to be healthy" without digging / Stays only on physical / Never references the goal later in the call.

**Auto-cap:** No North Star statement → max 10.

> **#1 loss dimension per Marcus's analysis:** Most coaches stay surface on the "why". They visit the goal but never strengthen the connection to the North Star.

### Dimension 5 — Program Explanation (3 Phases) (10 pts)

**What to look for:** Does the client leave understanding the 3-phase structure and why it exists?

> **Halden Method 3-phase canon:** **(1) Movement Retraining** — restore foundational movement quality, address asymmetries, retrain core patterns. **(2) Movement Remodeling** — load and rebuild new patterns into strength. **(3) Movement Integrating** — integrate full-capacity movement into life and sport.
>
> **Naming flexibility:** Accept ANY phrasing that conveys the three-stage progression in correct order. Legacy synonyms include Reset/Baseline → Build/Strength → Freedom/Mastery, or any equivalent (e.g. "rebuild the foundation → load the pattern → free movement"). Do NOT penalize a coach for using the canonical Retraining/Remodeling/Integrating naming.

| Band | Score | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **Elite** | 9–10 | All 3 phases clearly named (in any equivalent phrasing) with outcomes for each. Uses an analogy (pyramid, mountain, ladder) or reassessment cadence ("week 4, 8, 12, 16"). Each phase tied to the client's specific goal. High belief transfer — client understands WHY the path is structured this way. |
| **Strong** | 6–8 | All 3 phases clearly identified in correct order (with any naming), but delivery is simple or moderately generic. Phases may not be deeply tied to the client's specific goals, OR analogy / reassessment cadence is missing, OR the coach does not explicitly check client understanding. Score 8 when phases are crisp and complete; 6 when phases are present but explanation is brief. |
| **Mid** | 3–5 | Fragmented explanation. 1–2 phases mentioned vaguely, or the progression is implied but not laid out in sequence. Client may not clearly understand the journey. |
| **Weak** | 1–2 | Only references to "phases" or "steps" without naming or sequencing. |
| **Fail** | 0 | Skips or misrepresents. No phase explanation at all. Just "we'll do mobility exercises." |

**Positive signals:** "Movement Retraining → Remodeling → Integrating" (or any equivalent phrasing in correct order) / Analogies (mountain, pyramid, ladder) / Reassessment cadence stated / Phases linked to client's specific goal / Coach asks client if it makes sense.  
**Negative signals:** "We'll do a bit of everything" / No progression described / Phases named but not in the correct sequence / No tie to client goals.

> **Calibration note:** When a coach uses "Movement Retraining → Remodeling → Integrating" — that is the correct Halden Method naming. Credit it as Elite-tier phase identification. Do NOT drop the score because the labels don't say "Reset/Build/Freedom".

### Dimension 6 — Journey & Expectation Setting (10 pts)

**What to look for:** Does the coach prepare the client emotionally for the difficulty of the journey?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **10/10 — Elite** | Clearly explains milestones, timeline, challenges. Normalizes emotional friction explicitly: "There will be a week where you feel like you're not progressing — that's normal." Explains valleys (weeks 3–4). Distinguishes good discomfort from bad pain. Explains the first month is foundational, not transformational. Links back to North Star: "When we're at week 8, I'll ask if you feel like you're becoming…" |
| **7/10 — Strong** | Covers basics but misses emotional prep. Timeline explained, structure ok. Normalizes physical discomfort but not emotional. Missing psychological preparation for valleys. |
| **3/10 — Mid** | Vague expectations. Informative but not experiential. Explains what will happen but not how it will feel. Sounds instructional, not coaching. |
| **0/10 — Fail** | No expectation setting. Leaves client with unrealistic expectations. Doesn't mention that there will be hard moments. |

**Positive signals:** "Around week 3–4 you might feel in a valley" / "First month: foundational, not transformational" / "It's normal to feel overwhelmed — I want you to reach out when that happens."  
**Negative signals:** Only positivity, no mention of difficulty / No normalization of struggle.

> **#2 loss dimension per Marcus's analysis:** Most coaches explain the timeline but never normalize emotional friction. Clients hit week 3–4 unprepared and disengage.

### Dimension 7 — Support System Clarity (5 pts)

**What to look for:** Does the coach communicate, in this call, exactly how the client will be supported between sessions — primary channel, response expectations, community access, and how accountability will work?

This dimension scores **what is said in the call**. the training app message history is not required to score it: if the training app (or whichever support channel) is named, response times stated, the community platform/community mentioned, and accountability style discussed, the dimension can be scored fully. Set `disabled: false`.

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **5/5 — Elite** | Clearly explains all channels + when to use each. Primary channel named explicitly ("the training app is our main channel for daily check-ins"). Response time stated ("I respond within 24h"). the community platform/community access mentioned and how to use it. Accountability style asked or framed ("Do you want me to push you, or to stay in the background?"). Client visibly understands how to reach the coach and what to expect. |
| **3/5 — Mid** | Mentions support but unclear usage. Channel mentioned but no response times. Vague "reach out anytime" without structure. No accountability framing. |
| **0/5 — Fail** | Not explained. No mention of how the client reaches the coach between sessions. No channel named. No response expectations. |

**Positive signals:** "the training app is our main channel" / "I respond within 24h" / "the community platform community is where the cohort lives" / "Do you want me to push you or stay back?"  
**Negative signals:** No mention of the training app / Generic "reach out anytime" without structure / No response-time expectations / No accountability framing.

### Dimension 8 — Coaching Intelligence Questions (10 pts)

**What to look for:** Does the coach gather information that goes beyond logistics into behavioral patterns, psychology, and personalization?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **10/10 — Elite** | Asks key behavioral + self-awareness questions. Asks about behavioral patterns ("What has stopped you in the past?"). Probes consistency triggers ("What usually throws you off a program?"). Asks about learning style ("How do you learn best — do you need to be pushed or supported?"). Asks about stress response. Uses answers to personalize coaching approach. Identifies client archetype signals (Doer / Controller / Worrier / Follower). |
| **7/10 — Strong** | Asks 1–2 but lacks depth. Asks about pain triggers, schedule, training style. Missing behavioral pattern or mindset questions. Doesn't use answers to adapt approach. |
| **3/10 — Mid** | Generic questions only. Basic questions only (frequency, equipment, availability). Surface-level coaching. Doesn't use answers to adapt. |
| **0/10 — Fail** | Skipped. No coaching intelligence questions. Only "when are you available?" and "do you have equipment?" Client not truly known. |

**Positive signals:** "What has stopped you before?" / "How do you respond when you're overwhelmed?" / "Do you prefer I push you or support gently?" / "What usually makes you quit a program?"  
**Negative signals:** Only logistical questions / No psychological or behavioral inquiry / Doesn't use responses.

> **Archetype detection signals:** Doer = confident, data-driven, wants "the how". Controller = pushes back, questions the process, needs proof. Worrier = seeks reassurance, doubts themselves, asks "what if". Follower = enthusiastic but vague goals, history of not following through.

### Dimension 9 — Next Steps & Diagnostics (10 pts)

**What to look for:** Does the client leave knowing exactly what to do and when?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **10/10 — Elite** | Clear, confident, client understands exactly what to do. Pipeline: diagnostics → film → upload → program → start date. Explains how to film videos (angle, device). Removes all confusion with demo or screen share. Time specified ("send by Saturday, program Monday"). Client verbally confirms they understand. |
| **7/10 — Strong** | Some clarity but minor confusion. Clear instructions but no demo. Timeline ok but slightly rushed. Minor ambiguity but overall strong clarity. |
| **3/10 — Mid** | Vague instructions. Partially clear, some gaps. Client has unresolved doubts. No specific timeline. |
| **0/10 — Fail** | No clear next steps. "I'll send you some stuff" without explanation. Client doesn't know what to do after the call. No structure. |

**Positive signals:** "Here's what you do: open the training app, find diagnostics, film, upload" / "Send by Saturday, you'll have your program by Monday" / Screen share of apps.  
**Negative signals:** "I'll email you something" / Client asks "So what do I do now?" / No timeline.

> **#3 loss dimension per Marcus's analysis:** "I'll send you some stuff" is the most expensive sentence in coaching. Vague next steps lose clients in the hand-off.

### Dimension 10 — Booking Next Call (5 pts)

**What to look for:** Is the next call booked LIVE before the call ends?

> **Booking is verbal, not technical.** The substantive test is verbal confirmation of date and time during the call. Whether the calendar invite is technically clicked on-screen during the call versus immediately after is an artifact of the recording — it is NOT a deduction.

| Band | Score | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **Elite** | 4.5–5 | Date and time confirmed verbally during the call. Coach navigates the client's scheduling constraints live (time zones, weekly availability, conflicts). Score 5 when booking is crisp and the coach proactively closes it; 4.5 when booking is confirmed but slightly rushed or with minor ambiguity. |
| **Mid** | 2.5–3.5 | Attempted but not fully secured. Coach raises booking but leaves excessive flexibility ("I'll send you a link", "we'll organize via message") rather than locking date + time live. |
| **Weak** | 1–2 | Booking referenced only in passing, with no concrete attempt to lock it during the call. |
| **Fail** | 0 | Not addressed. Call ends without any mention of the next call. |

**Positive signals:** "Let's book it now before we close" / Specific date + time confirmed verbally / Resolves scheduling conflict live ("you're 13 hours ahead, so for you that's…") / "I've just sent your invite for…"  
**Negative signals:** "I'll send you the link" / Call closed without booking / "We'll figure it out via message."

> **Calibration note:** If date and time are confirmed verbally and any time-zone / scheduling friction is resolved live, score 5/5 — even if the transcript does not explicitly confirm that the calendar invite was sent during the recording.

> Marcus training: "Please, please, please — always book the next call before ending the meeting."

### Dimension 11 — Close, Recap & Confidence (5 pts)

**What to look for:** Does the call end with energy, structure, and an emotional anchor — not just logistics?

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **5/5 — Elite** | Strong recap, reinforces confidence. Structured recap: "Here's what we covered today: X, Y, Z." Confidence anchor: "You're in the right place" / "You're exactly the type of person who succeeds here." Emotional reinforcement: excitement for the journey. Does NOT end with only logistics. |
| **3/5 — Mid** | Basic close. Positive close but no structured recap. Generic encouragement without emotional anchor. Ends with next steps logistically. Or flat close: "Ok, talk soon." |
| **0/5 — Fail** | Abrupt or unclear ending. Call ends in a disappointing or flat way. Client leaves without feeling excited. |

**Positive signals:** "Here's what we covered…" / "You're in exactly the right place" / "I'm excited about this journey with you."  
**Negative signals:** "Ok, speak next time" / No summary / Flat or cold tone.

**Pattern note:** Even elite calls (Priya 94/100, Noor 91/100) typically score 3–4/5 here. Missing structured recap is the most universal gap across all coaches. Cap: no structured recap → max 3.

### Dimension 12 — Post-Call Execution (5 pts)

**What to look for:** Does the coach commit, in-call, to specific post-call deliverables with concrete deadlines? This dimension scores **what is said in the call** — verification of actual delivery is out of QC scope until the training app + the video tool integration is live.

> **Informal commitments still count.** A specific commitment with implied or rough timing (e.g. "I'll get your diagnostics done over the weekend") is a real promise — score it in the Mid band, not Fail. Reserve Fail for calls where the coach makes no commitment at all.

| Band | Score | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **Elite** | 4.5–5 | Multiple explicit post-call commitments with precise deadlines: recap timing ("recap within 10 minutes"), diagnostics assigned live ("assigning your diagnostics in the training app now"), program delivery date stated ("program loaded by Monday"). All time-bound. Score 5 when 3+ commitments with crisp timing; 4.5 when 2+ commitments with precise timing. |
| **Strong** | 3.5–4 | Two or more post-call commitments with mostly precise timing — minor gaps (one commitment with rougher timing, or one specific commitment plus one general one). |
| **Mid** | 2–3 | At least one specific commitment, but timing is rough or only one or two commitments are made. Includes informal-but-real commitments like "I'll get diagnostics done over the weekend". Partial in-call promise hygiene. |
| **Weak** | 1 | Vague reference to follow-up ("I'll send you stuff") without specific deliverable or timing. |
| **Fail** | 0 | No post-call commitments stated at all. Call ends with no clarity on what the coach will do next. |

**Positive signals:** "I'll send you the recap in the next 10–15 minutes" / "I'm assigning diagnostics now" / "Your program will be ready by Monday" / Specific or implied timing on every commitment / Concrete deliverable named.
**Negative signals:** No mention of follow-up actions / "I'll send you stuff" without specifics / No timing on any commitment / No concrete deliverable named.

> **Calibration note:** A commitment like "I'll get the diagnostics done over the weekend" is real but soft — score it as Mid (2–3), not Fail (0).

> ⚠️ **Scope note:** This dimension scores **what is said in the call**. Verification of actual post-call delivery (the video tool sent, recap email landed, diagnostics assigned in the training app) is out of QC scope until the integration is live. Set `disabled: false`. Score the in-call promise quality.

## Scoring Bands Reference

| Band | Score | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **ELITE** | 90–100 | Deep + clear + client confirms. Coach builds real human relationship, not just process. |
| **STRONG** | 80–89 | Clear and useful but lacks emotional depth or consistent reinforcement. |
| **INCONSISTENT** | 70–79 | Technically correct but generic or surface-level in key areas. |
| **AT RISK** | 60–69 | Weak client experience. Client may be doubting the program. |
| **FAIL** | < 60 | Missed core elements, major retention risk. |

## Scoring Principles

1. **Every score is based on 3 things:** depth (how far the coach goes), clarity (how well it lands), and client response (did it actually land).
2. **If it doesn't land → it cannot score elite.** A perfect explanation that the client didn't confirm is not elite.
3. **Quote-first rationale.** Every rationale must begin with a direct or paraphrased reference to something that happened in the transcript. Never score from impressions.
4. **Conservative on missing evidence — within the band.** If a behavior cannot be verified in the transcript, score in the lower tier of the band the call belongs to. This is NOT a license to drop into a lower band entirely. If the call clearly exceeds Mid criteria but you cannot verify every Elite element, score in the Strong band (lower tier) — do not collapse to Mid.
5. **The four-sentiment test.** Before finalizing each score, ask whether the client would leave the call feeling:
   1. "This coach gets me"
   2. "I know exactly what to do next"
   3. "I trust this process"
   4. "I'm excited to start"

   Not: "That was informative" / "I think I understand."

## Calibration Anchors (Reviewer-Aligned Patterns)

---

The patterns below are drawn from real reviewer corrections (anonymized). Use them to keep your scoring centered on the reviewer's reference standard. When you encounter a similar pattern, score consistent with the corrected value, not the original.

**D1 — credit content over disclosure.**
- Coach references the client's specific goals + injuries from the sales call but does NOT say "I read your notes" → Strong (7–8), not Mid. The conduct is the test, not the disclosure.
- Coach is otherwise well-prepared but makes one factual misstep (e.g. wrong city) → Strong (6–7), not Mid. A single error in an otherwise solid opening is not a band drop.
- Coach lets the client share context voluntarily for connection / relational warmth → not a deduction. This is an intentional technique, not a preparation failure.

**D3 — natural agenda phrasing is Elite.**
- Coach says "we've got 30 minutes — connect, get aligned on your goals, what success looks like, walk you through the journey, get clear on support, and schedule the next call" → 4.5–5, not 3. Sequenced delivery + time framing + ≥3 phases qualifies as structured, even without numbered enumeration.

**D5 — accept the canonical Halden Method naming.**
- Coach explains "Movement Retraining → Movement Remodeling → Movement Integrating" → credit as fully-named 3-phase explanation. Score in Strong–Elite range based on depth, analogies, and tie-to-goals — NOT based on whether the labels match "Reset/Build/Freedom".

**D10 — verbal booking is Elite.**
- Coach confirms specific date and time verbally and resolves time-zone or scheduling friction live → 5/5, even if the transcript does not explicitly verify the calendar invite was sent during the recording.

**D12 — informal commitments are Mid, not Fail.**
- "I'll get your diagnostics done over the weekend" → 2–3, not 0. Commitment exists with implied timing; it is real even if soft. Reserve 0 for the absence of any commitment whatsoever.

> When your initial scoring instinct lands on Mid and the call shows clear evidence of exceeding Mid criteria, re-check against these anchors before finalizing. The most common failure mode is collapsing borderline cases into the Mid bucket — the Strong band exists precisely for them.
